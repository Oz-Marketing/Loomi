Three reports covering the same question from different angles: when somebody
nearby looks for a business like this one, what do they find, and what do they
do about it?

# Business Profile

How the listing performed: how often it appeared, in search and in maps, and
what people did next — called, asked for directions, visited the website.

Direction requests are the underrated number. Somebody asking for directions has
made a decision, and it is about as close to a visit as a digital signal gets.

Requires a connected Business Profile for the account.

# Reputation

Review volume and rating over time, across the sources connected for the
account.

Two things matter more than the average:

- **Recency.** A 4.6 average built two years ago reads as a 4.6 from two years
  ago.
- **Response rate.** Answered reviews — especially the bad ones — measurably
  affect whether people call.

A sudden run of poor reviews is almost always an operational event with a date
attached. Find the date first.

# Call tracking

Calls from tracked numbers, with source and duration.

Duration is the quality signal. A high volume of very short calls usually means
one of three things, and it is worth working out which: the number is being
called by robocallers, the ads are attracting the wrong intent, or nobody is
answering the phone.

:::note
Call tracking only counts calls to tracked numbers. Somebody who saw the ad and
dialed the number on the building is a real result this report cannot see.
:::
