This group connects marketing activity to what the business actually did. They
depend on outcome data being available for the account — where it isn't, the
report says so rather than showing a confident zero.

# Acquisition cost

Marketing spend against customers acquired, over the period.

Read it as a trend. A single month's cost per acquisition swings on timing —
spend lands in one month, the sale closes in the next — and the trend is what
survives that.

# Lead performance

Leads by source, and what happened to them. The useful comparison is not which
source produced the most leads but which produced the most that went anywhere.

A source with half the volume and three times the conversion is the better
source, and volume-only reporting hides that completely.

# Sales trend and service trend

Volume over time, with the comparison period alongside.

Look for the shape rather than the number. Seasonality is real in most of these
businesses, and a March that is down on February but up on last March is a good
March.

# Service retention

Whether service customers come back, and how the gap between visits is moving.

This is the report most worth a slow read. Retention moves quietly and it moves
late, and by the time it shows up in sales it has been happening for a year.

# Customer heatmap

Where customers are, geographically. Useful for two decisions: where to target
paid media, and where a conquest campaign has room to work.

The interesting part is usually the gap — an area with population, no
competitor, and no customers.

# Direct mail

Response and return for mail campaigns, matched back against customer records
where the data allows.

:::note
Every report in this group is only as good as the outcome data behind it. If
sales or service records are not flowing for an account, these reports narrow
to what Loomi can see on its own. That is a data connection question, not a
reporting one.
:::
